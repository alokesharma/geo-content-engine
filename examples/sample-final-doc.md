# How to Choose a Standing Desk

A standing desk is worth buying when it is stable at your typing height, lifts the load you put on it, and holds its position without drifting. The rest is preference. This guide covers the five things that decide whether a desk is good for you: height range, stability, motor type, desktop size, and warranty.

## What to check before you buy

Start with height range. Measure your elbow height while standing, then confirm the desk reaches it. Most sit-stand desks cover roughly 28 to 48 inches, which suits people from about 5'4" to 6'1". If you are shorter or taller than that band, check the exact numbers before you order.

Then check the rated load. A desk rated for 150 lbs handles a monitor arm, two displays, and a laptop with margin. A desk rated near your actual load will feel slower and may strain over time.

## Single vs dual motor

A single-motor desk lifts from one leg and drives the other through a crossbar. It costs less and rises slower, and it can lean under an uneven load. A dual-motor desk drives both legs, lifts faster, and stays level. If you load one side more than the other, the dual motor is the safer pick.

Motor noise comes up often in owner threads. Dual-motor desks tend to run quieter under load because neither motor works at its limit.

## Stability and wobble

Wobble is the most common complaint. It grows with height, so test the desk at its tallest setting, not its lowest. A wide foot, a thick crossbar, and a heavier frame all reduce sway. If you type hard or use an ultrawide monitor, prioritise the frame over the desktop.

## Warranty and what it covers

Read what the warranty covers, not just its length. A 5-year warranty on the frame and 2 years on the motor is common. Confirm the motor and the electronics are both included, because those are the parts that fail.

## FAQ

1. **What height range fits most people?**
   About 28 to 48 inches covers roughly 5'4" to 6'1". Confirm the exact range against your measured elbow height.

2. **Is a dual motor worth the extra cost?**
   If you load the desk unevenly or want a faster, quieter lift, yes. For a light and balanced load, a single motor is fine.

3. **How do I reduce wobble?**
   Buy a wider foot and heavier frame, and test stability at full height before you commit.

## Key Takeaways

- **Height range first.** Match the desk to your measured elbow height.
- **Check the rated load.** Leave margin above your actual weight.
- **Dual motor for uneven loads.** It lifts level, fast, and quiet.
- **Test wobble at full height.** That is where instability shows.
- **Read the warranty terms.** Confirm the motor and electronics are covered.
